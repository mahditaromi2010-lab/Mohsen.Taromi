Hiring an overseas truck driver through a DAMA

Short on drivers in the NT or regional WA? Here is how a transport business can sponsor an experienced driver like Mohsen, in plain English.

What a DAMA is

A Designated Area Migration Agreement lets employers in a regional area sponsor overseas workers on terms that standard visas do not offer, such as concessions on age (under 55) and a lower English requirement.

How it works in five steps

  1. Show the needDemonstrate that you cannot fill the truck driver role locally.
  2. Get endorsedThe region's Designated Area Representative endorses you for Truck Driver (General), ANZSCO 733111.
  3. Sign a labour agreementAgree terms with the Department of Home Affairs.
  4. Nominate the driverNominate a named worker for the role.
  5. Visa and arrivalThe driver applies for the visa and travels to you.

Questions employers ask

Is Mohsen ready to relocate?
Yes. He lives in Shiraz today and is ready to move to regional Australia, such as the Northern Territory or Western Australia.
Which licence does he hold?
An Iranian Class 1 heavy goods licence (over 6 tonnes), held since 1997 with a clean record. An Australian HR/HC assessment will be completed on arrival.
What is his English level?
Basic. He is studying for the IELTS test now, as the visa requires.
Can he work in extreme heat and in remote areas?
Yes. He has spent years in Bandar Abbas, where summers pass 40 °C, and long remote roads are normal work for him.
Can I see his documents?
On request. His licences, employment records, training certificates and passport are never published online.
Which visa could he come on?
Through a labour agreement such as a DAMA, usually the Skills in Demand (482) or the regional (494) visa, which can later lead to permanent residency. Your Designated Area Representative or a registered migration agent confirms which one applies to you.
How does his Iranian licence become an Australian one?
He expects to sit the standard theory and driving tests for a local licence first, then the heavy vehicle (HR/HC) assessment. Rules differ by state and change, so he will follow the NT or WA licensing authority's current steps. He plans to complete this on arrival.
Can we interview him online?
Yes, at any time that suits you. Email him and he will reply with times and, on request, his documents.

This is general information, not migration advice. Confirm details with the Designated Area Representative or a registered migration agent (https://www.mara.gov.au).
